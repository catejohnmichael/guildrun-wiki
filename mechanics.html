Stats

Defense

How much incoming damage is reduced.

How much incoming damage is reduced.

damage taken = raw ÷ (1 + Defense/100) · (negative Defense: raw × (1 + |Defense|/100))
Worked exampleA hero with 133 Defense hit for 400: 400 ÷ (1 + 133/100) = 400 ÷ 2.33 = 172 taken — about 57% less. Defense has no cap and never fully blocks a hit; it just keeps scaling.
DefenseIncoming ×Reduction
0÷ 10% less
50÷ 1.533% less
100÷ 250% less
133÷ 2.3357% less
200÷ 367% less
300÷ 475% less
Applies to
  • Auto-attack hits
  • Ability & projectile damage
  • Rift-storm damage
Not affected
  • Burn, Poison & Bleed (status damage ignores Defense)
  • Damage already absorbed by a shield
Crit and non-crit portions of a hit are reduced separately, and the game uses integer math. Negative Defense (from Frost or debuffs) makes you take more.

Base Attack Damage vs Attack

Your auto-attack hit, and the % stat that boosts it.

Your auto-attack hit, and the % stat that boosts it.

auto-attack damage = BaseAttackDamage × (1 + Attack/100)
Worked exampleThe ✊ Base Attack Damage stat is the real hit. Attack (🔨) is only a % bonus on top: BaseAtkDmg 40 with 25 Attack → 40 × 1.25 = 50 per auto. Most heroes start at Attack 0, which is why you see a big fist number next to Attack 0.
Applies to
  • Auto-attacks only
Not affected
  • Ability damage (abilities scale off Attack through their own “% Attack” factors, not this formula)

Magic

Spell power — inert until an ability uses it.

Spell power — inert until an ability uses it.

ability damage = BaseDamage + Σ (statValue × rank factor)
Worked example200 Magic on its own does nothing. It only matters when an ability says it scales with Magic — e.g. “275 (+250% Magic)” at that point adds 250% of your Magic to the hit.
Applies to
  • Abilities that list a “% Magic” scaling
Not affected
  • Auto-attacks
  • Any ability that doesn’t scale with Magic
Same is true for how abilities read Attack, Crit, Defense etc. — the ability text tells you which stats it uses.

Attack Speed

How fast you auto-attack.

How fast you auto-attack.

attacks/sec = BaseAttackSpeed × (1 + AttackSpeed/100), clamped to 0.2–5 · timer = 1 ÷ that
Worked exampleBase 0.40/s with 50 Attack Speed → 0.40 × 1.5 = 0.60/s, i.e. an auto every 1.67s. The rate is hard-capped between 0.2 and 5 attacks per second no matter how much you stack.
Applies to
  • Auto-attack rate
Not affected
  • Ability cast speed / cooldowns
Frost lowers Attack Speed by 0.5 per stack.

Crit

Crit chance, and bonus crit damage past 100.

Crit chance, and bonus crit damage past 100.

crit chance = min(Crit, 100) / 100 · crit damage = 2 + max(Crit − 100, 0)/100
Worked example50 Crit = 50% chance to deal ×2 damage. 150 Crit = 100% chance and ×2.5 damage: the first 100 buys the guaranteed crit, every point after adds +1% crit damage.
Applies to
  • Auto-attacks
  • Abilities flagged as able to crit
Not affected
  • Abilities not marked critable
  • Status (Burn/Poison/Bleed) ticks

Mana

How abilities are paid for.

How abilities are paid for.

+5 mana per auto-attack · +ManaRegen every 2s
Worked exampleYou gain 5 mana on every auto-attack plus your ManaRegen tick, then cast when full. Right after a cast a hero’s ManaLockTime briefly blocks mana gain, so faster attackers with high ManaRegen cast far more often.
How it interacts
  • Casting frequency

Attack Range

Melee vs ranged.

Melee vs ranged.

Range 1 = melee · Range > 1 = ranged
Worked exampleA hero with Range 4 attacks from four hexes away; Range 1 must be adjacent. Distances are measured on the hex grid.

Damage

⚔️

The damage pipeline

The order every hit is processed in.

The order every hit is processed in.

crit roll → crit multiply → Defense → damage immunity → shields → health, then omnivamp heals the attacker
Worked exampleA critting auto for 100 base: ×2 crit = 200, ÷ Defense, then it eats the target’s shield first and only the remainder hits health. Finally the attacker heals for Omnivamp% of what actually landed.
Status damage (Burn/Poison/Bleed) skips the crit and Defense steps but is still absorbed by shields.

Omnivamp & HP/s

Lifesteal and flat regen.

Lifesteal and flat regen.

heal = final damage dealt × Omnivamp/100 · HP/s = flat health per second
Worked exampleWith 20 Omnivamp, a hit that lands 150 after Defense & shields heals you for 30. HP/s is separate: a flat trickle every second regardless of whether you’re attacking.
How it interacts
  • Omnivamp: scales off damage that actually lands
Omnivamp uses post-Defense, post-shield damage — the number you actually dealt, not the raw hit.

Shields

A temporary health buffer.

A temporary health buffer.

shields absorb before health · last 10s unless the source says “lasting”
Worked exampleA 200 shield in front of 1000 HP means the next hits chew through the 200 first. Even Burn/Poison, which ignore Defense, are absorbed by a shield.
Applies to
  • Absorbs all incoming damage, including status ticks
Not affected
  • Wears off after 10s unless the effect is marked lasting

“True damage” & Damage Amp don’t exist yet

A common tooltip trap.

A common tooltip trap.

Worked exampleNothing in this build actually deals true damage or applies Damage Amp — the stats are in the code but nothing reads them. If an item promises “true damage,” treat it as cosmetic for now.
Verified by disassembly: the true-damage actions and the DamageAmp/BaseTrueDamage/BonusTrueDamage getters have zero call sites. Worth re-checking each patch.

Status effects

Burn

Stacking fire damage that decays.

Stacking fire damage that decays.

1 damage per stack every 1s, then −1 stack
Worked example8 Burn deals 8 damage now, drops to 7, deals 7 in 1s, and so on — a front-loaded burst that fades over 1s-steps. Ignores Defense.
How it interacts
  • Ignores Defense entirely
  • Shields DO absorb it — a shielded target takes no Burn until the shield is gone
Damage per tick equals the current stack count; each tick then removes one stack.

Poison

Stacking damage that doesn’t decay.

Stacking damage that doesn’t decay.

1 damage per stack every 2s · no decay
Worked example10 Poison deals 10 every 2s and keeps going at full strength until the fight ends or it’s cleansed — unlike Burn, stacks don’t tick down. Ignores Defense.
How it interacts
  • Ignores Defense entirely
  • Shields DO absorb it — Poison ticks eat the shield first, then health
Because it never decays, Poison out-damages Burn over a long fight; Burn wins the opening seconds.

Frost

A slow / soften debuff.

A slow / soften debuff.

−0.5 Defense and −0.5 Attack Speed per stack · −1 stack every 2s
Worked example20 Frost = −10 Defense and −10 Attack Speed on the target, decaying one stack every 2s. Enough Frost pushes Defense negative, so hits land for more.
How it interacts
  • Lowers the target’s Defense and Attack Speed
  • Deals no damage by itself
💫

Stun

A short hard crowd-control.

A short hard crowd-control.

1.5s base · being stunned grants +25% stun resistance (stacks, 15s)
Worked exampleA stun holds a hero for 1.5s. Each stun then stacks +25% stun resistance for 15s, so chain-stunning the same target gives shorter and shorter stuns.

Bleed & Heal-over-time

The remaining timed effects.

The remaining timed effects.

Bleed: 1 damage per stack · Heal-over-time lasts 6s · AntiHeal ×0.5 healing
Worked exampleAntiHeal halves all incoming healing (×0.5) while active — a hard counter to Omnivamp and regen builds. Heal-over-time effects pay out over 6s.

Combat & run

🎯

Who enemies attack (targeting & threat)

Default target selection.

Default target selection.

target = the NEAREST enemy (hex-grid distance) · ties: class resistance → lowest effective HP · some enemies use threat = (25·Attack + 25·Magic + 20·Attack Speed + 10·Crit + 2·ManaRegen) ÷ 5
Worked exampleBoard position decides who gets hit. Every unit picks the enemy closest to it on the hex grid, so whoever you place nearest the enemy line tanks — put the tank in front and the carry at the back. Only if two targets are at the same distance do the tie-breaks (class resistance, then lowest effective HP) matter. A few enemies use the highest-threat rule instead, which picks your biggest Attack/Magic damage dealer wherever it stands.
How it interacts
  • Nearest-enemy is the default for heroes and enemies alike
  • Targets are sticky until they die or become untargetable
  • Taunt and a few abilities override the choice
Not verified: whether an exact-distance tie prefers a particular board slot (e.g. top-left). The tie-break order above is carried over from an earlier build.
📏

Size (“grow in size”)

Cosmetic only — does nothing in combat.

Cosmetic only — does nothing in combat.

hero model scale only · the size value is clamped to 0–1 and read solely by the renderer
Worked exampleRelics and abilities like Frontline Embiggener read “gain 400 Max HP and grow in size.” Only the 400 Max HP does anything — “grow in size” just scales the hero’s model on the board. Stack several size-ups and the model gets visibly bigger (up to a cap), but the hero hits, tanks, blocks, and is targeted exactly the same.
Applies to
  • The on-screen size of the hero model — visual feedback for stacking buffs
Not affected
  • Stats — HP, Defense, Attack, Magic, everything
  • Targeting & threat (still nearest-enemy by hex distance)
  • Pathing & blocking — each unit occupies exactly one hex no matter how big it looks
  • Hitbox, attack range, and AoE overlap
Verified by disassembly (build 25323618; unchanged in 25461680): ModifySizeAction adds its Delta to a per-character “size increase” value; SetCharacterSizeIncrease clamps it to 0–1, stores it in the sim frame, and fires OnCharacterSizeIncreaseChangedEvent. The one and only subscriber is the character view controller, which tweens the model’s localScale (capped by MaxHeroScaleFactor). No stat, targeting, or pathfinding code reads the value. Worth re-checking each patch.
🌀

Rift storm (the fight timer)

What ends a stalled fight.

What ends a stalled fight.

starts at 50s · 5+ damage every 1s, +5/tick · turns multiplicative (×1.5/tick) after another 15s
Worked exampleDrag a fight past 50s and the rift storm begins: 5 damage, then 10, then 15… every 1s. After another 15s the growth turns multiplicative (×1.5 each tick) and spirals fast. Unlike Burn/Poison, storm damage does go through Defense.
How it interacts
  • Reduced by Defense
It’s a soft enrage timer: win before it snowballs. Rift-breaker enemies use their own harsher values.

Rank-up stat gains (C → B → A → S)

How ranking a hero up scales its stats.

How ranking a hero up scales its stats.

per-rank gain = genericRate + ⌊primary-or-secondary rate ÷ number of classes⌋, all × (rank − 1)
Worked exampleStats are listed at rank C. A single-class Assassin gains +19 Crit per rank (generic 4 + Assassin’s primary 15). A Tank+Assassin only gets +11 Crit (4 + ⌊15 ÷ 2⌋), because the class bonus is split across both classes.
Max HP scales by percent (+12.5% generic, +25% primary); the other stats are flat. A stat a class doesn’t list gains only the generic rate.

Shard generation

Why “gain N shards” fires effects once.

Why “gain N shards” fires effects once.

one shard event = one trigger carrying the amount — not N separate procs
Worked example“Gain 4 shards” fires the OnShardsGenerated trigger once with value 4, not four times. So a relic like Shard Power Extractor (10% enemy Max HP per generation event) procs once for that whole payout — proc count depends on how often you gain shards, not how much.
Effects that want the amount use the “per trigger value” action family; others are flat per event.

Stat & notation key

What the table columns and the shorthand inside ability/relic text mean. Damage and scaling logic verified against the disassembly (see MECHANICS_VERIFIED).

ColumnMeaning
HPMax health.
DefDefense. Incoming damage is divided by (1 + Def/100) — 100 Def ≈ half, 200 ≈ a third. Negative Def amplifies damage instead.
BaseAtkDmgBase auto-attack damage (the ✊ fist stat) — the hero's actual auto-attack hit before modifiers.
AttackA % modifier on auto-attacks: auto damage = BaseAtkDmg × (1 + Attack/100). Usually 0 at base — a bonus stat, not the damage source, which is why most heroes show Attack 0 next to a non-zero BaseAtkDmg.

Rank scale: C → B → A → S = ranks 1 → 4 (C is the base/floor at which stats are listed). Events' "rank 1–4" and the rank→rarity table use the same numbers.

Per-rank brackets: a|b|c|d after a stat or effect is its value at rank C|B|A|S. e.g. "150 + 100|200|400|600% Attack" = 150 base damage plus Attack scaled 100 / 200 / 400 / 600% at rank C / B / A / S.

Ability shorthand: "N% Stat" adds N% of that stat to the hit (ability damage = BaseDamage + Σ statValue × rank factor); "Stat/N" is that stat ÷ N (a trailing ×M multiplies the result); "1dN" is a random roll of 1…N.

Technical formulas (verified)

The exact combat & shop math, read straight from the compiled simulation code — the player-facing cards above are the plain-English version of these.

Show combat & shop formulas

Everything below was read from the compiled simulation code (Ember.Simulation.Core), not inferred from play. All stats are 16.16 fixed point internally; ints are truncated where noted. Function names are the real ones from the binary.

Stat value

Every stat is a CharacterStat with BaseValue, RankBonus, ConnectedStatBonus, ConstantBonus, TemporaryBonus, ConstantPercentageBonus and TotalPercentageModifier. Its value is

value = ( BaseValue + (RankBonus + ConnectedStatBonus + ConstantBonus + TemporaryBonus) × (1 + ConstantPercentageBonus) ) × (1 + TotalPercentageModifier)

(CharacterStat.get_Value, inlined everywhere.) Items' flat stats land in ConstantBonus; "+X% stat" effects in the percentage fields.

Attack and Attack Speed are percentages of a base

  • Auto-attack damage = BaseAttackDamage stat, whose ConnectedStatBonus = BaseAttackDamage.BaseValue × Attack / 100 (truncated to int). So a hero with Base Atk Dmg 43 and Attack 25 hits for 43 + 43×0.25 = 53 before defense. (CharacterStatsComponent.RecalculateConnectedStats, AutoAttackHandler.BuildPreparedAttack)
  • Attacks per second = BaseAttackSpeed stat, whose ConnectedStatBonus = BaseAttackSpeed.BaseValue × AttackSpeed / 100, then clamped to [MinAttacksPerSecond, MaxAttacksPerSecond] = [0.2, 5]. The attack timer is 1 / that. (Entity.RefreshAttackTimer, HeroesSingleton)
  • Magic is not connected to anything by default; abilities read it through their own scaling factors.

Damage pipeline (auto attacks, abilities, projectiles, effect damage)

  1. Crit roll: chance = min(Crit, 100) / 100, rolled with the deterministic RNG (CharacterStatsComponent.GetCrit). Abilities only roll if the source's AbilitiesCanCrit is set or the action is "critable".
  2. Crit multiplier = 2.0 + max(Crit − 100, 0) / 100. Every Crit point above 100 is +1% crit damage. (GetCritDamageMultiplier) On a crit the OnGlobalCrit trigger fires with the bonus crit damage.
  3. Defense (target): if Defense > 0, damage = damage / (1 + Defense/100); if Defense < 0, damage = damage × (1 + |Defense|/100). Integer division, so 100 Defense halves damage, 200 Defense cuts it to a third. (DamageHandler.CalculateDamageAfterDefense) Both the crit and non-crit amounts are mitigated separately so the log can show the mitigated part.
  4. Damage immunity: a target with the DamageImmunity status takes 0 (the ApplyDamage call still happens with 0).
  5. Shields first: StatusComponent.DealShieldDamage absorbs into shields; the remainder is subtracted from Health. Shield removal fires OnShieldRemovedFromDamage. (DamageHandler.ApplyDamage)
  6. Omnivamp: the attacker heals final (post-defense, post-shield-accounting) damage × OmniVamp / 100. (GetOmniVampHeal, then HealingHandler.Heal)

Status damage (Burn, Poison, Bleed) skips Defense entirely: DamageHandler.DealStatusDamage goes straight to ApplyDamage, so shields still absorb it but Defense does not reduce it. Rift-storm damage does go through Defense (DealStormDamage).

DamageAmp, BaseTrueDamage and BonusTrueDamage exist as stats and have helper functions (GetDamageAmpedDamage = damage × (1 + amp/100); GetTrueDamage = damage × X/100), but in this build no combat path reads them: the only readers of those three stat slots are their own getters/setters and the bonus-adding helpers. Treat any item text that promises "true damage" or "damage amp" as not yet wired up, and re-check after each patch.

Ability damage

damage = BaseDamage + Σ over scalings ( stat value × RankFactor[hero rank] )

then the same crit → defense → shield pipeline (DealCritableDamageWithScalingAction, DamageScaling.GetFactorForRank, Entity.GetStatScaling). The factors per rank are shown on each ability as Rank1Factor…Rank4Factor (rank C, B, A, S). Description text like 250 + 300% Attack is exactly BaseDamage 250 with an Attack factor of 3.0.

Status effects (StatusSingleton values)

  • Burn: BurnDamagePerStack (1) × stacks every BurnDamageInterval (1 s), then one stack is removed.
  • Poison: PoisonDamagePerStack (1) × stacks every PoisonDamageInterval (2 s); stacks do not decay on their own.
  • Frost: −FrostDefenseReductionPerStack (0.5) Defense and −FrostAttackSpeedReductionPerStack (0.5) Attack Speed per stack; one stack dissipates every FrostDissipationInterval (2 s).
  • Stun: StunDuration 1.5 s base; being stunned gives heroes +StunResistanceBonusAmount (25%) stacking for StunResistanceBonusDuration (15 s).
  • Shields last 10 s unless "lasting". AntiHeal multiplies healing by AntiHealReductionFactor (0.5).
  • Mana: ManaGeneratedPerAttack (5) per auto attack, plus ManaRegen every GlobalManaRegenInterval (2 s); ManaLockTime blocks mana gain right after a cast.

Rift storm (BattleSingleton)

Starts at SecondsUntilStormStart (50 s). Every DamageInterval (1 s) it deals DamagePerInterval (5), increasing by DamageIncreasePerInterval (5) each tick; after SecondsUntilExponentialStorm (15 s more) the increase becomes multiplicative by ExponentialIncreasePerInterval (1.5). Storm damage is reduced by Defense. Rift-breaker enemies use the RiftBreaker* values instead.

Rank-up

Each rank adds the "generic per rank" flat bonuses to every stat, plus the larger primary-stat bonus for the class's primary stat and the secondary bonus for its secondary stats (HeroesSingleton, applied in UpdateRankBonuses as RankBonus / percentage). See the table on the Classes page.

Shop odds — what "chance" means and how the pity works

Per shop build the game takes the chunk's base chances (ShopSingleton tables on the Shop page), adds a per-rank smoothing offset, prints them to the log, and builds a weighted list:

weight(rank) = 100 × (base[chunk][rank] + smoothing[rank])   — entries with weight ≤ 0 are simply not added
P(slot shows rank X) = weight(X) / Σ weights

Each of the 3 hero slots (2 item, 1 relic slots) rolls independently from that list (WeightedRandomList.GetRandom). Then:

  • When a rank is rolled into a slot, ResetOddSmoothing zeroes its smoothing and removes that extra weight from the list immediately, so the remaining slots of the same shop use the base chance.
  • After the shop is built, SmoothChancesFromSales adds the chunk's "increase" value to the smoothing of every rank/rarity that did not appear in the offered choices. This happens on every build, including rerolls.
  • Negative base values are a floor: e.g. chunk-0 Rank A starts at −10 with +2.5 per roll, so it cannot appear until the 5th consecutive roll without an A (−10 → −7.5 → −5 → −2.5 → 0 → 2.5), and then shows at 2.5/(85+2.5+B) ≈ 2.8% while B's pity is 0.
  • Verified against 300+ shop lines in the local logs: chunk 0 hero lines go 85/0/−10/0 → 85/10/−7.5/0 → 85/20/−5/0 … and drop back exactly when the rank appears; item Epic climbs −10, −5, 0, 5 … and relic Epic −5, 0, 5, 10 …

Per-floor rank increases (HeroesSingleton.RankChanceIncreasePerFloor) are all 0 in this build, so the floor itself does nothing; only the chunk (act half) and the pity counters matter. The super shop uses its own base tables and the same mechanism.

Worked example, chunk 0, fresh run, first shop: weights C 8500, B 0, A ignored, S 0 → every slot is Rank C (100%). After one shop with no B: B pity 10 → second shop P(B per slot) = 10/95 = 10.5%, P(at least one B in 3 slots) = 1 − 0.895³ ≈ 28%.
